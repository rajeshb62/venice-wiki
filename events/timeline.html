Chronological Timeline of Venice

A master chronology from the lagoon settlements through the fall of the Republic. Entries in bold are covered by dedicated wiki pages. Local terms are given in Italian/Venetian/Latin where they illuminate the entry.

Origins and Byzantine Vassalage (c.400–1000)

DateEvent
421Traditional founding date of Venice (25 April, feast of San Marco). Fugitives from the Visigothic sack of Aquileia settle the lagoon islands at Rialto. The date is legendary; continuous settlement probably dates to the 6th century.
568Lombard (Longobardi) invasion of northeastern Italy drives another wave of refugees into the lagoon. The island communities become permanent.
c.697First doge (from Latin dux, “leader”) elected, traditionally Paolo Lucio Anafesto. Venice remains nominally a province of the Byzantine Empire.
726Venice begins to assert autonomy from Byzantium during the Iconoclasm crisis. The lagoon communities act collectively against imperial tax collectors.
810Capital (caput) of the Duchy of Venice moved from Malamocco to the more defensible Rialto island cluster — the nucleus of the future city.
828Relics of San Marco evangelista (Saint Mark the Evangelist) stolen from Alexandria (al-Iskandariyya) by Venetian merchants. The translatio — the theft — gives Venice its patron saint and political legitimacy. The first Basilica of San Marco is built to house the relics.
1000Doge Pietro II Orseolo leads a naval expedition to pacify Dalmatian pirates (pirati) and liberate Adriatic ports. The expedition inaugurates the annual Sposalizio del Mar (“Marriage to the Sea,” also called Festa della Sensa — from Ascensione, Ascension Day). See adriatic-lordship.

Rise of the Commercial Republic (1000–1262)

DateEvent
1082Emperor Alexios I Komnenos issues the chrysobull (χρυσόβουλλον, chrysoboullon, “golden seal decree”) granting Venetian merchants free trade throughout the Byzantine Empire with full exemption from all customs duties — privileges denied to Byzantine merchants themselves. This is the foundation of Venetian commercial supremacy in the East.
1095–99First Crusade (Prima Crociata). Venice profits by providing naval transport and supplies; extracts trading rights in crusader ports.
1171Emperor Manuel I Komnenos arrests all Venetians in Byzantine territory and confiscates their goods — estimated at 100,000 pounds of gold. Venice attempts a retaliatory fleet expedition; it fails catastrophically and the doge Vitale Michiel II is assassinated on his return. The episode eliminates Venice’s Byzantine trade privileges for a generation and shapes the Fourth Crusade agenda.
1202–04Fourth Crusade (Quarta Crociata). Venice transports the crusading army under an agreement (pactum) to be paid 85,000 silver marks. When the crusaders cannot pay, Doge Enrico Dandolo redirects the fleet first to sack Zara (Zadar, a Venetian rival under Hungarian protection) then to Constantinople. The city is stormed and sacked (April 1204). Venice receives three-eighths of the Byzantine Empire including Crete, the Peloponnese ports (Modon, Coron), Euboea (Negroponte), and the Ionian Islands.
1204The Latin Empire of Constantinople is founded. Venice holds the Quartier (Ruga Veneta, “Venetian street”) in Constantinople and controls key straits. The colonial thalassocracy (θαλασσοκρατία, “sea-rule”) is established.
1261Emperor Michael VIII Palaiologos reconquers Constantinople with Genoese help, signing the Treaty of Nymphaeum (1261). Venice loses its Byzantine privileges; Genoa (Genova) gains them. The Venetian-Genoese rivalry intensifies.
1262Monte Vecchio (“old mountain/fund”) established — the first consolidated public debt (debito pubblico) fund. Outstanding forced loans (prestiti) are aggregated into a single funded debt paying 5% interest. This is the foundation of Venetian public finance. See public-debt.
1284The ducato d’oro (“gold ducat”) introduced by the Senate. Weight: 3.545 g, fineness 99.47% gold. The ducat becomes the reference currency of Mediterranean trade for five centuries.
1297Serrata del Maggior Consiglio (“Closing of the Great Council”) — the constitutional reform restricting Great Council membership to a hereditary patrician class. The Libro d’Oro (“golden book”) records eligible noble families. Venice becomes an oligarchic republic. See constitutional-development.

The Black Death and Recovery (1310–1381)

DateEvent
1310The Tiepolo–Querini conspiracy (congiura) attempts to overthrow the oligarchy. It fails; the Council of Ten (Consiglio dei Dieci) is established as a permanent security court. See council-of-ten.
1338Venice acquires Treviso — the first permanent terraferma (“firm land”) possession.
1348The Morte Nera (Black Death, Pestilenza) strikes Venice. An estimated 60% of the population dies within 18 months. The lagoon’s geography makes containment slightly easier than on the mainland, but the demographic shock is severe.
1380–81War of Chioggia (Guerra di Chioggia) — Venice vs. Genoa. The Genoese fleet blockades Venice in its own lagoon; the Republic appears on the verge of extinction. A brilliant naval counteroffensive traps the Genoese fleet at Chioggia. The Peace of Turin (1381) ends the war; Venice is victorious but financially exhausted. Genoa never recovers as a maritime power. See war-of-chioggia.

Imperial Expansion and the Terraferma (1381–1453)

DateEvent
c.1390Venice begins systematic mainland expansion. The Republic’s traditional aversion to land empire (dominium terrae) gives way to strategic necessity as overland trade routes and the Alpine passes require political control.
1395Tana (Azov), Venice’s Black Sea trading post, sacked by Tamerlane (Timur Lenk). See tana.
1400Damascus (ash-Shām, دمشق) sacked by Tamerlane. The Levant spice trade is disrupted. See damascus.
1401First major banking panic (fallimento bancario) on the Rialto. Several deposit banks fail amid the financial strain of the Levant disruptions and war debts. See bank-failures-and-panics.
1404–09Venice acquires Padua (Padova, 1405), Verona (1405), Vicenza (1404), and much of Istria and Dalmatia. The Dominio di Terraferma takes shape.
1415Second Rialto banking panic. See bank-failures-and-panics.
1416Venetian fleet defeats the Ottomans at the Battle of Gallipoli (Gelibolu) — the first major naval engagement between Venice and the Ottoman Empire.
1423Francesco Foscari elected doge — beginning the longest dogeship of the 15th century (34 years). He presides over the peak of terraferma expansion. See francesco-foscari.
1423The Lazzaretto Vecchio (“old lazaretto”) established on an island in the lagoon — the world’s first permanent plague quarantine facility. See science-and-medicine.
1441Venice briefly acquires Ravenna; the terraferma reaches its maximum extent.
1454Peace of Lodi (Pace di Lodi) ends the Lombard Wars. The five major Italian states (Venice, Milan, Florence, Papacy, Naples) agree to a balance of power under the Lega Italica (“Italian League”). A generation of relative peace follows. See peace-of-lodi.
1456Third major Rialto banking panic. See bank-failures-and-panics.
1457Doge Foscari is forced to abdicate by the Council of Ten — the only time in Venetian history a doge was deposed. He dies eight days later. See francesco-foscari.

Ottoman Pressure and Cultural Zenith (1453–1503)

DateEvent
1453Fall of Constantinople (Caduta di Costantinopoli) to Sultan Mehmed II (al-Fātiḥ, “the Conqueror”). Venice loses its Quartier in Constantinople and its Black Sea access. Greek scholars and manuscripts flee to Venice, fuelling the Renaissance. See fall-of-constantinople.
1468Cardinal Bessarion (Βησσαρίων, metropolitan of Nicaea) donates 752 Greek and Latin manuscripts to Venice — the foundation of the Biblioteca Marciana (bibliotheca Sancti Marci, “Library of Saint Mark”). See cardinal-bessarion, biblioteca-marciana.
1470Fall of Negroponte (Chalcis, Euboea) to the Ottomans after a failed Venetian naval relief operation. The loss shocks Europe. See negroponte.
1474First printing privilege (privilegio) granted by the Venetian Senate — to Johannes von Speyer’s heirs, for printing technology. Venice’s printing industry begins its explosive growth.
1482–84War of Ferrara. Venice overreaches against the Papacy and is forced to return Ferrara. The terraferma empire stalls.
c.1485Fra Mauro’s world map (mappamondo) enters Venetian collections. Commissioned by King Afonso V of Portugal, the 1459 original is lost; a Venetian copy survives in the Biblioteca Marciana. See fra-mauro.
1489Cyprus (Kypros, Κύπρος) acquired via the abdication of Queen Caterina Cornaro — a Venetian noblewoman — who hands the kingdom to the Republic. Venice controls the eastern Mediterranean’s last major crusader kingdom. See cyprus.
c.1494Aldus Manutius establishes the Aldine Press (in aedibus Aldi, “at the house of Aldus”) in Venice. He begins the systematic publication of Greek classical texts in accurate editions. See the-aldine-press, aldus-manutius.
1495–99The Italian Wars (Guerre d’Italia) begin with the French invasion of Charles VIII (1494). Venice initially benefits from the chaos; later it becomes a primary target of the European coalition.
1496Aldus receives a Senate privilegio for his Greek typeface (carattere greco) — the first Greek-type monopoly. See the-aldine-academy-and-greek-printing.
1497Giovanni Cabot (Zuan Chabotto, John Cabot) — a Venetian-born mariner in English service — sails from Bristol and reaches Newfoundland (Terra Prima Vista, “first seen land”), claiming North America for England. See giovanni-cabot.
1498Vasco da Gama rounds the Cape of Good Hope (Cabo da Boa Esperança) and reaches Calicut (Kozhikode) in India. The Portuguese Cape route begins to bypass the Venetian spice monopoly through Alexandria and Beirut. The Muda di Alessandria begins its long decline. See the-spice-trade.
1499Aldus publishes the Hypnerotomachia Poliphili — the most beautifully illustrated book of the 15th century. See the-hypnerotomachia-poliphili.
1499Battle of Zonchio (Sapienza) — first major sea battle between Venice and the Ottoman fleet. Venice loses.
1499–1500Great banking panic (fallimento generale): multiple Rialto banks fail simultaneously amid the Ottoman war and Portuguese spice disruption. The Garzoni bank — operating for nearly 70 years — collapses. The Rialto private banking system never fully recovers. See bank-failures-and-panics, the-garzoni-bank.

The League of Cambrai and Recovery (1503–1540)

DateEvent
1501Aldus introduces the italic type (carattere corsivo, “running/cursive character”) designed by francesco-griffo and the pocket octavo format with the Virgil edition. The portable book is born.
1501Leonardo Loredan becomes doge (–1521). His portrait by Giovanni Bellini becomes the iconic image of the Renaissance doge. See leonardo-loredan.
1503Peace with the Ottomans. Venice cedes several Aegean outposts but retains Cyprus, Crete, and Corfu.
1508Erasmus visits Venice and works in Aldus’s print shop, preparing the expanded Adagia — his great collection of Greek and Latin proverbs. See erasmus-in-venice.
1508League of Cambrai (Lega di Cambrai) formed — Pope Julius II, Emperor Maximilian I, France (Louis XII), Spain, Ferrara, Mantua, and Florence unite against Venice. It is the most formidable coalition ever assembled against the Republic. See league-of-cambrai.
1509Battle of Agnadello (Ghiaradadda) — 14 May. The League’s forces crush the Venetian army; the terraferma collapses. Venice briefly retains only the lagoon islands. The crisis is the greatest in the Republic’s history since Chioggia.
1510–16Venice systematically recovers the terraferma by exploiting divisions in the League. Pope Julius II switches sides (1510); France and the Empire begin fighting each other.
1516Ghetto established (29 March) in the Cannaregio sestiere — the enclosed Jewish quarter, the first in Europe. Jews are required to live on the island of the old foundry (geto vecchio/geto nuovo) and wear identifying marks. See the-ghetto-and-jews-of-venice.
1516Peace of Brussels effectively ends the League of Cambrai. Venice recovers most of the terraferma.
1517Martin Luther posts the Ninety-Five Theses — the Reformation begins. Venice initially tolerates Protestant ideas, then comes under increasing Inquisition pressure.
1523Andrea Gritti becomes doge (–1538), presiding over the post-Cambrai cultural and architectural renaissance. See andrea-gritti.
1527Sack of Rome (Sacco di Roma) by Imperial and Spanish troops. Many Roman artists, scholars, and architects flee to Venice, fuelling a new cultural boom (Sansovino, Aretino, Titian).
1532The Flanders Galleys (muda di Fiandra) make their last voyage. The route is no longer profitable; Dutch and English shipping has taken over northern European trade. The muda system begins its terminal decline. See the-muda-system.
1538Battle of Preveza — Ottoman admiral Hayreddin Barbarossa defeats the Holy League fleet; Venice loses further Adriatic and Greek posts.

Decline, Culture, and Ottoman Wars (1540–1645)

DateEvent
1540Venice loses Nauplia (Napoli di Romania) and Monemvasia (Malvasia) to the Ottomans under the peace treaty ending the Third Ottoman-Venetian War.
c.1545Titian paints the Pope Paul III with his Grandsons — perhaps the most psychologically acute portrait of the Renaissance. See titian.
c.1548–94Tintoretto (Jacopo Robusti) paints the Scuola Grande di San Rocco cycle — 50+ canvases filling the entire building. The largest single painting programme by one artist in history. See tintoretto.
1570–71The Ottomans invade Cyprus. After a long siege, Famagusta (Ammochostos) falls (August 1571); the Venetian commander Marcantonio Bragadin is flayed alive. See cyprus.
1571Battle of Lepanto (Battaglia di Lepanto, 7 October) — the Holy League (Venice, Spain, Papacy) destroys the Ottoman fleet in the Gulf of Patras. The largest naval battle in Mediterranean history: ~400 ships, ~140,000 men. The Venetian galleasses (galeazze) — heavy artillery platforms — are decisive. See battle-of-lepanto.
1573Venice makes a separate peace with the Ottomans, formally ceding Cyprus and paying 300,000 ducats. Spain is furious.
1575–77Plague (pestilenza) kills an estimated 50,000 Venetians — nearly a third of the city’s population. Titian dies (27 August 1576), probably of plague. The Redentore church is built as a thanksgiving vow.
1587Banco della Piazza di Rialto (banco publico, “public bank”) established — the world’s first publicly chartered transfer bank operating on 100% reserves. It provides a stable monetary foundation after the collapse of the private banchi di scritta. See the-banco-della-piazza-di-rialto.
1588The Spanish Armada (Grande Armata) is defeated by England. The shift of maritime power to the Atlantic accelerates.
1606–07Interdict of Venice (Interdetto) — Pope Paul V places Venice under interdict after the Republic refuses to surrender two clerics to ecclesiastical justice. Venice, advised by the Servite friar Fra Paolo Sarpi, orders clergy to continue sacraments and expels the Jesuits. The Interdict is lifted without substantive Venetian concession. See interdict-of-1606-07, paolo-sarpi.
1619Banco del Giro (banco del giro, “circulation bank”) established, replacing the Banco della Piazza. The Banco Giro becomes Venice’s central monetary institution for the remainder of the Republic.

The Long Decline (1645–1797)

DateEvent
1645Cretan War (Guerra di Candia) begins — the Ottoman Empire invades Crete (Candia, Κρήτη) with 60,000 men. The siege of the capital Candia (Herakleion) lasts 21 years — the longest siege in history. See cretan-war.
1669Candia falls to the Ottomans (6 September) after the withdrawal of French relief forces. Venice loses Crete after 465 years of rule. It is the death blow to Venetian colonial power.
1684–99Morea Campaign (Conquista della Morea) — Francesco Morosini (il Peloponnesiaco) reconquers the Peloponnese (Morea) for Venice. In 1687, artillery fire from Morosini’s guns strikes the Parthenon in Athens — used as an Ottoman powder magazine — and explodes it. Venice briefly holds Athens. See morea-campaign.
1699Treaty of Karlowitz (Karlovitz) — Venice regains the Peloponnese and some Aegean islands in the first significant Ottoman territorial concession. A brief recovery.
1715The Ottomans retake the Peloponnese. Venice cannot resist.
1718Peace of Passarowitz (Požarevac) — Venice loses the Peloponnese permanently and retains only Corfu, the Ionian Islands, and a few Dalmatian ports. The Venetian Empire is functionally over.
1797Fall of the Republic (Caduta della Serenissima). Napoleon Bonaparte’s Italian campaign reaches the Veneto. On 12 May 1797 — after 1,100 years — the Great Council votes to accept Napoleon’s ultimatum and dissolves itself. The last doge, Ludovico Manin, removes his corno ducale (“ducal cap”) and hands it to his servant, saying Tolè su, no la dopero più (Venetian: “Take it, I shan’t be needing it any more”). The Treaty of Campo Formio (October 1797) hands Venice to Austria. See fall-of-the-republic.
