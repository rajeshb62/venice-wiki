Venice: 15th and 16th Century

A narrative research wiki on the Venetian Republic during its golden age and gradual decline — the period of its greatest maritime power, territorial expansion, Renaissance cultural flowering, and eventual struggle against the Ottoman Empire and the shifting European order.

Entities

People

  • Andrea Gritti — doge 1523–38, hero of the Padua recapture, post-Cambrai recovery.
  • Francesco Foscari — doge 1423–57, longest-serving 15th-c doge, mainland expansion, Two Foscari tragedy.
  • Leonardo Loredan — doge 1501–21, League of Cambrai crisis, Bellini portrait.
  • Pietro Bembo — humanist, poet, official historian, linguistic theorist.
  • Marin Sanuto — diarist, author of the 58-volume Diarii (1496–1533).
  • Fra Mauro — cartographer, creator of the great 1459 world map.
  • The Polo Brothers — Niccolo, Matteo, and Marco; journey to Kublai Khan.
  • Giovanni Cabot (John Cabot) — Venetian navigator, 1497 Newfoundland voyage for England.
  • Cardinal Bessarion — Greek scholar, donor of library (foundation of Biblioteca Marciana).
  • Francesco Carmagnola — condottiero, executed by Venice for suspected betrayal.
  • Francesco Sforza — condottiero, became Duke of Milan, former Venetian Captain-General.
  • Gattamelata (Erasmo da Narni) — condottiero, Donatello’s bronze equestrian statue.
  • Bartolomeo Colleoni — condottiero, Verrocchio’s equestrian statue, Colleoni Chapel.
  • Aldus Manutius — printer and scholar, founder of the Aldine Press, Neacademia, Greek classics, italic type, octavo revolution. ⮕ aldus-manutius
  • Francesco Griffo — punchcutter and type designer, creator of the first italic type for Aldus. ⮕ francesco-griffo
  • Paolo Sarpi — Servite friar, historian, Adviser in Theology, defender of Venice during the Interdict of 1606–07, author of the History of the Council of Trent. ⮕ paolo-sarpi
  • Erasmus in Venice — the 1508 visit, Aldine Adagia, workshop conditions with Aldus. ⮕ erasmus-in-venice
  • Titian — Vecellio, the most complete expression of the Renaissance in art, Assunta, Pesaro Madonna, portraits, late poesie. ⮕ titian
  • Tintoretto — Jacopo Robusti, religious intensity, Scuola di San Rocco, Paradise, dramatic light and foreshortening. ⮕ tintoretto
  • Giovanni Bellini — greatest Venetian master of the 15th century, devotional Madonnas, Dead Christ, San Zaccaria altarpiece. ⮕ giovanni-bellini
  • Giorgione — creator of the easel picture, the Giorgionesque spirit, The Tempest, Sleeping Venus. ⮕ giorgione
  • Paolo Veronese — joyous worldliness, Marriage at Cana, Feast in the House of Levi, decorative splendour. ⮕ paolo-veronese
  • Carpaccio — master of pageant pictures, St. Ursula cycle, confraternity narrative works, documents of Venetian life. ⮕ carpaccio
  • Tiepolo — the last great Venetian painter, frescoes in Wurzburg, Palazzo Labia, Ca’ Rezzonico. ⮕ tiepolo
  • Canaletto — master of the veduta, Grand Canal views, English patrons, shaped the visual image of Venice. ⮕ canaletto

Places and Institutions

  • The Arsenal — Europe’s largest industrial complex, shipbuilding centre.
  • Biblioteca Marciana — Republic’s great public library, founded on Bessarion’s 1468 bequest of 752 Greek and Latin manuscripts; housed in Sansovino’s Piazzetta masterpiece. ⮕ biblioteca-marciana
  • Fondaco dei Tedeschi — German merchant quarter near Rialto, silver & copper trade, banking, Fugger connection, Aldine book distribution. ⮕ fondaco-dei-tedeschi
  • Candia/Crete — Venetian colony since 1204, sugar, lost 1669.
  • Negroponte (Euboea) — major Aegean colony, lost to Ottomans 1470.
  • Cyprus — acquired 1489 via Caterina Cornaro, lost 1570–71.
  • Corfu — Venetian possession 1386–1797, key Adriatic sentinel.
  • Rialto — commercial and banking centre, the bridge.
  • The Lido — barrier island, port channel.
  • Antwerp — rising 16th-century commercial centre, Portuguese spice competition, silver flows, Venetian consulate. ⮕ antwerp
  • London — Flanders galleys terminus, exchange and banking hub (Priuli, Borromei, Barbarigo), Aldine book distribution. ⮕ london
  • Constantinople — Venetian quarter and bailo, trade nexus, Fall of 1453, post-Ottoman diplomacy, source of Greek manuscripts for Aldus. ⮕ constantinople
  • Genoa — Venice’s great maritime rival, War of Chioggia, competition in the East, exchange triangles. ⮕ genoa
  • Bruges — Flanders galleys terminus, northern exchange market, Borromei company, shift to Antwerp. ⮕ bruges
  • Padua — first major terra firma acquisition (1405), University, Gritti’s recapture (1509), Gattamelata. ⮕ padua

Trade Route Cities

  • Alexandria — primary terminus of the Red Sea spice route, destination of the Alexandria muda, Venetian purchasing agents. ⮕ alexandria

  • Aleppo — Syrian terminus of the Persian Gulf caravan route, Venetian consulate, silk trade. ⮕ aleppo

  • Damascus — northern Red Sea caravan terminus, Venetian fondaco and consulate, Tamerlane’s destruction (1400). ⮕ damascus

  • Cairo — Mamluk capital, bottleneck of the Red Sea spice route, Venetian diplomatic missions. ⮕ cairo

  • Beirut — Mediterranean port for Syrian caravan routes, destination of the Beirut muda. ⮕ beirut

  • Tana — Venetian outpost on the Sea of Azov, Black Sea spice route, sacked by Tamerlane (1395). ⮕ tana

  • Trebizond — Black Sea terminus of the Persian overland route, Greek empire, birthplace of Bessarion. ⮕ trebizond

  • Aden — Red Sea transshipment port, Indian Ocean spice reloading point, rival to Jiddah. ⮕ aden

  • Ormuz — Persian Gulf entrepot, Mongol-era spice route terminus, captured by Portuguese (1507). ⮕ ormuz

  • The Scuole Grandi — confraternities, charity, art patronage.

  • The Great Council — sovereign body, Serrata of 1297.

  • The Council of Ten — secret police, political intelligence.

  • The Senate (Pregadi) — executive body, the Collegio, the Savi.

  • The Grain Office — quasi-public bank, grain provisioning, depository for terra firma lords. ⮕ the-grain-office

  • The Garzoni Bank — non-noble banking family, 1430–1499, failure after seven decades. ⮕ the-garzoni-bank

  • The Soranzo Fraterna — noble family bank, failed twice after Fall of Constantinople. ⮕ the-soranzo-fraterna

  • The Datini Archive — Francesco Datini’s papers in Prato, 7,000 exchange rates. ⮕ the-datini-archive

  • The Sopraconsoli — commercial magistracy, bankruptcy supervision. ⮕ sopraconsoli

  • Avogaria di Comun — state advocates, prosecuting magistrates. ⮕ avogaria-di-comun

  • Procuratori di San Marco — trust administrators, estate records for Monte Vecchio. ⮕ procuratori-di-san-marco

  • The Priuli Fraterna — noble banking family, exchange market with London. ⮕ the-priuli-fraterna

  • The Aldine Press — the most influential Venetian printing house, founded c.1494 by Aldus Manutius, Torresani, and Barbarigo; octavo format, italic type, Greek classics. ⮕ the-aldine-press

  • The Interdict of 1606–07 — Venice vs Pope Paul V, Paolo Sarpi, defiance.

  • The Fall of the Republic (1797) — Napoleon, Campo Formio, end of 1,100 years of independence. ⮕ fall-of-the-republic
