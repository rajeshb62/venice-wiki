This is the second and final volume of Hazlitt’s comprehensive history of Venice. It covers the Republic’s history from 1457 to its final dissolution in 1797, followed by 25 thematic chapters on Venetian civilization, government, economy, culture, and daily life. The volume includes plates, maps, corrigenda, and a full index.

The 15th and 16th century chapters (XXXII-XXXVI) cover Venice’s peak maritime power — the acquisition of Cyprus, the League of Cambrai, the Battle of Agnadello, the recovery of Padua, the Battle of Marignano, the Battle of Lepanto, and the Interdict controversy with Pope Paul V — as well as cultural figures like Titian, Paolo Sarpi, and Bianca Cappello.

NARRATIVE HISTORY (Chapters XXXII-XLI)

CHAPTER XXXII — A.D. 1457–1501 (p. 1)

Venice in the mid-15th century — Dominio Veneto replaces Commune Venetiarum — the Turks at Constantinople — a new crusade organized and abandoned (1464) — succession of Doges (1462–1501) — the Fire of 1479 — quarrel with the Holy See over Ferrara — interdict — acquisition of Cyprus — Caterina Cornaro’s last days — geographical discoveries and Venetian anxiety — the French in Italy — the Holy League — Ludovico il Moro — Battle of Sapienza (1499) — Venetian defeat.

CHAPTER XXXIII — A.D. 1501–1509 (p. 38)

Leonardo Loredano, Doge — formation of a European coalition against Venice — League of Cambrai — friendly feeling of Henry VIII toward Venice — his diplomatic offices at the Vatican — offers of help from Scotland and Spain — Henry VIII and the Venetian ambassador — Cardinal Wolsey’s interviews with the Venetian envoy.

CHAPTER XXXIV — A.D. 1509–1529 (p. 50)

The League of Cambrai — vigilance and activity of the Republic — defeat at Agnadello — arrival of the intelligence at Venice — launch of the Interdict by Julius II — fortitude of the Government — the Doge sends his plate to the Mint — recovery of Padua (17 July 1509) — repulse of Emperor Maximilian — defection of Julius II — Leo X (1513) — reconciliation with France — Battle of Marignano decided by the Venetians (1515) — Charles V — Battle of Pavia (1525) — Italian League against the Emperor (1526) — sack of Rome (1527) — Treaty of Cambrai (1529) — obstacles to Italian unity.

CHAPTER XXXV — A.D. 1521–1573 (p. 94)

Death of Leonardo Loredano (1521) — splendid funeral — Andrea Gritti, Doge (1523–38) — Turkey and Egypt — absorption of Egypt by the Porte — plague and famine — convalescent homes — Gritti’s architectural improvements — Council of Trent — Protestantism and Elizabeth of England — explosion of the Venetian powder magazine at the Arsenal (1569) — loss of Cyprus (1570) — Battle of Lepanto (1571) — rejoicings at Venice — peace with Turkey (1573).

CHAPTER XXXVI — A.D. 1545–1607 (p. 127)

Sequence of Doges (1545–77) — Henry III of France at Venice (1574) — his magnificent reception — plague of 1576 and death of Titian — the Pope sends the Golden Rose — great Fire of 1577 — loss of treasures — story of Bianca Cappello as Grand Duchess of Tuscany — quarrel with Pope Paul V (1605–07) — Fra Paolo Sarpi appointed Canonist and Ritualistic Adviser (1606) — European sympathy with Venice — Interdict and its withdrawal — triumph of the Republic over the Curia — James I and his Apology for the Oath of Allegiance (1607) — plot to assassinate Sarpi.

CHAPTER XXXVII — A.D. 1607–1623 (p. 160)

Spanish Conspiracy against Venice (1618) — long and secret preparations — the city full of traitors and bravoes — denunciation to the Signory — wholesale arrests — 300 executions — French and Spanish ambassadors leave Venice — death of Ossuna — case of Antonio Foscarini (1622) — Lady Arundel and Sir Henry Wotton — mistake of the Council of Ten — death of Paolo Sarpi (1623).

CHAPTER XXXVIII — A.D. 1623–1669 (p. 201)

Rialto Bridge erected (1588) — Doge Bembo — Reniero Zeno’s attacks on the Council of Ten (1624–28) — Venice and the Netherlands — War in the Valtelline (1620) — plague at Venice (1630) — deaths of Richelieu and Louis XIII (1642–43) — Candiot War (1644–69) — strenuous exertions to preserve Crete — Treaty of Westphalia (1648) — eventual surrender of Candia to the Porte on honourable terms.

CHAPTER XXXIX — A.D. 1669–1699 (p. 240)

Operations in the Morea under Francesco Morosini — successes — election of Morosini to the Dogeship (1688) — his death at Napoli di Romania (1694) — victories over the Turks at Zenta by Prince Eugene (1697) — Peace of Carlowitz (1699).

CHAPTER XL — A.D. 1699–1762 (p. 263)

War of the Spanish Succession — Venetian neutrality — loss of the Morea — Treaty of Passarowitz (1718) — Venice adopts permanent neutrality — relaxation of customs tariff (1736) — “a great Empire reduced to a great City” — Clement XIII, a Venetian, becomes Pope (1758).

CHAPTER XLI — A.D. 1762–1797 (p. 284)

Marco Foscarini, Doge (1762–63) — Angelo Emo’s triumphs at sea (1784–92) — case of Pier Antonio Gratarol — Paolo Renier and Luigi Manin, the last Doges (1779–97) — approach of the end — the French in Italy — negotiations of Procurator Pietro Pesaro with Bonaparte — last act of the Venetian Senate (1797) — surrender of the city to the French — the 120th Doge abdicates.
